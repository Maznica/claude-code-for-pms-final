PLOT TWIST!

One query unmasked the villain

Seconds between pings

If someone says no, the gap shouldn't change. If nobody answers, it should move by exactly what the timer moved.

Exactly 30 seconds less, and only when nobody answered. Case closed!

THE HERO'S CODE

Six rules I learnt the hard way

1

CONSENSUS IS A HYPOTHESIS

Three senior people agreed on the cause. One chart showed they were all wrong. Ask what would prove us wrong.

2

THE AI CAN BE WRONG. SO CAN I.

I changed my mind four times on the record, once by pushing back on the AI's reading of the code. I was right.

3

A METRIC CAN LIE NICELY

Putting the timer back brings acceptance back to ~76% while four people stay at zero. Pair every KPI with who it hides.

4

READ THE CODE. REALLY.

232 lines. Not scary with a sidekick explaining them. It settled a question engineering had open for three weeks.

5

WRITE IT DOWN EVERY SESSION

Each session ended by saving what we found to CLAUDE.md. Session six could still cite, and correct, session one.

6

CHECK THE AUTOMATION

A sample report claimed 4 flags. The real run found 9, and the sample was older than the folder it described.

POWER MOVES

Three prompts you can steal tomorrow

THE GAP FINDER

What's contradictory or missing across the wiki, the database and the files in my repo?

Finds where sources disagree. That's usually where the answer is.

THE EITHER/OR

Is the decline caused by users ignoring notifications, or by notifications failing to reach them?

Two mechanisms, no room for a story. A question that can be answered wrong is worth asking.

THE PUSHBACK

I disagree with you that [X]. From what I've seen, [Y]. What am I missing?

Treat it like a sharp colleague: one who can be wrong, and who has to show you.

YOUR ORIGIN STORY STARTS HERE

How you start: no coding needed

PICK ONE REAL QUESTION

Not a demo. A metric that moved, a ticket theme you can't explain, a brief you don't trust.

GIVE IT A WORKING CONTEXT

A CLAUDE.md file with what you know, who owns what and what's still open. It's the sidekick's memory between sessions.

CONNECT YOUR SOURCES

Wiki, database, tickets, code. Most of the answer came from combining them.

CHECK EVERY ANSWER

Ask for the query, the line of code, the source. If it can't show you, it doesn't count.

MAKE IT REPEATABLE

If you do it twice, turn it into a skill the whole team can run.

THE 30-DAY CHALLENGE

My proposal for our team:

Week 1: everyone picks one open question from their area.
Week 2: run it with an AI sidekick, and save the prompts.
Week 3: show-and-tell. What did it get wrong, and how did you catch it?
Week 4: one person turns their best workflow into a team skill.

Best catch of the month wins the X-Ray Vision award.
