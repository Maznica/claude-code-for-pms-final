Bulk Callout - "One-pager, Product, Dispatch, 12 January 2026"
  owner named .................. NO - flagged
  success measure .............. yes
  scope stays bounded .......... yes
  problem stated before fix .... yes
  evidence dated and current ... NO - flagged
  -> 2 flags.

     1. No owner named. The brief says so itself: "nobody's picked it
        up yet," and "Flagging this for whoever picks it up next
        quarter." The two parties it does mention are roles, not
        people - "the product designer" and "engineering" - and naming
        a function is not assigning responsibility. Nobody is on the
        hook for this brief.

     2. No evidence, and ten months elapsed. The brief is dated 12
        January 2026; it is now 7 November. Its entire evidentiary
        basis is one undated anecdote, "Halloran mentioned it too, on
        the Supply side," and it carries no figures at all - not how
        often multi-responder incidents happen, not how long the
        sequential flow takes, not how many handlers hit it. The one
        proposed metric is explicitly un-baselined: "we don't have a
        clean baseline for it yet." Ten months on, nothing in the
        brief lets a reader judge whether the problem is still live or
        how big it ever was.

        Criterion note: criterion 5 is written for figures that are
        undated or stale. It does not cleanly cover a brief that rests
        on no figures at all. Judged against the criterion's purpose -
        can a reader tell whether the evidence supports acting now -
        this is a miss, and a brief resting on nothing is weaker than
        one resting on dated evidence. The criterion's wording should
        be amended to say so explicitly.

     Note on the success measure, which passes: it is the weakest pass
     here. "Fewer support tickets mentioning a handler who 'just
     started calling people directly'" is a named, countable change in
     behaviour, and "time-to-full-coverage ... should drop" names a
     real metric. Neither carries a target, and the brief admits the
     baseline is missing - but it says what to measure and flags the
     gap, which is what the criterion asks for.

     Note on scope, which passes well: the Problem section raises
     Halloran's Supply-side version of the same need, and the Scope
     section then rules it out by name - "no bulk requisitions." A
     neighbouring need raised and explicitly excluded is bounded
     handling, not creep.
