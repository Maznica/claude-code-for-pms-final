Handler Phone App - "One-pager, Product, Dispatch, 8 September 2026"
  owner named .................... NO - flagged
  success measure ................ yes
  scope stays bounded ............ yes
  problem stated before fix ...... NO - flagged
  evidence present and current ... NO - flagged
  -> 3 flags.

     1. No owner named for the work proposed. There is an Owner
        section, but it reads "The product designer, exploring for Q4
        alongside the console work. Not yet staffed for a build." A
        role is identified, no person or team is named, and the build
        - which is what this brief asks for - is explicitly unstaffed.
        Exploration has an owner; the proposal does not.

     2. Solution before problem. The brief opens "## Proposal - We
        should build a phone app for handlers," and describes
        notifications, status view and one-tap contact before saying
        what is wrong. The problem appears in the second section,
        "What this solves," and even there it arrives as a feature
        request rather than a problem: Aunt Dot asking for "something
        that tells me too."

     3. Evidence: the whole case rests on one anecdote. A single
        interview quote from one handler, "a handler in the interview
        pile," with no figures of any kind - not how often handlers
        miss a status change, not how many handlers are affected, not
        how long the gap lasts. The brief says so itself: "No baseline
        number yet." It is the most recent of the four briefs (two
        months old), so age is not the problem here; absence is.

Requisition Approval Chains - "One-pager, Product, Supply, 14 July 2026"
  owner named .................... yes
  success measure ................ yes
  scope stays bounded ............ NO - flagged
  problem stated before fix ...... yes
  evidence present and current ... NO - flagged
  -> 2 flags.

     1. Scope grows mid-document, and the brief draws the line itself
        before crossing it. The Proposal states the ask and closes it:
        "Add a second approval step for requisitions over $2,000...
        That's the whole ask." The very next sentence picks up four
        more things - route around a slow approver after 48 hours, a
        dashboard of every pending approval across all three sites,
        handler-visible requisition status, and replacing "the weekly
        gear-cage spreadsheet Halloran keeps by hand." The Open
        questions section confirms it is unresolved: "How much of the
        above ships together versus in phases - TBD."

        Note: the success measure passes, but it measures only the
        original ask - requisitions over $2,000 shipping without a
        second sign-off. None of the four additions has a measure.
        The ask grew; the measure did not.

     2. Evidence: no figures behind the problem. The $2,000 threshold
        is a policy parameter the brief proposes, not evidence for it.
        Nothing says how many requisitions exceed $2,000, how often,
        or whether a single-approval purchase has ever gone wrong.
        The case rests on one anecdote - "Halloran's asked more than
        once for a second set of eyes."

Routing Override Audit Log - "One-pager, Product, Dispatch, 5 March 2026"
  owner named .................... yes
  success measure ................ NO - flagged
  scope stays bounded ............ yes
  problem stated before fix ...... yes
  evidence present and current ... NO - flagged
  -> 2 flags.

     1. No success measure. The brief has no such section. The
        Proposal says what gets recorded - "who made it, when, which
        responder they picked instead of the recommended one, and a
        short reason if they typed one" - and where it appears, the
        console's existing activity view. That is a description of
        what gets built and surfaced, which the criterion explicitly
        does not accept. Nothing says how anyone would know it was
        working: not how often overrides happen, not whether the
        support lead's team can close a complaint faster, not
        anything checkable.

     2. Evidence: no figures, and eight months elapsed. Dated 5 March,
        read 7 November. The case rests on "The support lead's team
        has hit this more than once" - no count of complaints, no
        count of overrides, nothing a reader can re-run today.

     Note on the owner, which passes: "The engineering manager's team
     builds it" names a role rather than a person, but it assigns the
     proposed work to one specific, identifiable team with a start
     condition ("once profile redesign wraps"). That is the line this
     run drew between this brief and Handler Phone App, where the role
     named is exploring and the build is unstaffed. The test is
     whether the work the brief proposes has somebody on the hook, not
     whether a proper noun appears.

OUTSIDE THE CHECKLIST - the five criteria do not cover this, and it is
recorded here rather than as a flag.

  Routing Override Audit Log describes itself as "ready to build" and
  says the work "can pick this up once profile redesign wraps." Rook's
  release history has profile redesign AND a routing override audit
  log both shipping in 4.0 on 7 April 2026. If that is the same work,
  this brief has been describing finished work for seven months, and
  every flag above is a flag on a historical document.

  The checklist cannot see this. It grades briefs as documents and has
  no criterion for whether the work already happened. Worth confirming
  against the 4.0 release notes before anyone acts on the two flags.
